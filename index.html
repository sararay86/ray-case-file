01Executive Summary
The case that should not exist.

Sara Ray — a pro se defendant since August 2025 — has been under criminal prosecution in Weehawken Municipal Court for a petty disorderly persons offense for over 590 days. The maximum possible penalty is a $500 fine. Seventeen hearings scheduled. No trial date has ever been set.

The complainant claimed to have video evidence. The prosecutor confirmed in writing that no such video exists. The complainant claimed to be out of the country on a key court date. Gym footage and key fob records place him in the building that day. The judge acknowledged the delay on the record and expressed indifference.

The Charges
Disorderly Conduct (N.J.S.A. 2C:33-2) and Harassment (N.J.S.A. 2C:33-4). Maximum penalty: $500 fine.

Key Claims

  • First Amendment Retaliation — complaint filed in direct response to Sara exercising legal rights
  • 42 U.S.C. § 1983 — state actors sustaining a baseless prosecution, denying speedy trial, due process, equal protection
  • Speedy Trial Violation — N.J. Const. Art. I §10; State v. Szima, 70 N.J. 196 (1976)
  • Malicious Prosecution & Abuse of Process — complaint filed as leverage to extinguish Mukamal's own criminal exposure
  • Private Prosecutor Without Authority — R. 7:8-7(b) compliance never confirmed on the record
  • Recusal DENIED — All Allegations Unrebutted — at April 22 hearing, recusal was denied but judge did not rebut any substantive allegation on the merits
  • Court Steering Defendant Away from Constitutional Ruling — judge is actively blocking a speedy trial ruling to prevent a with-prejudice dismissal and the resulting lawsuit foundation

Why This Requires Retained Counsel

  • The pending charges affect Sara's ability to work in her industry, where positions require security clearance — dismissal on the merits is non-negotiable
  • The record is built: ACJC complaint, recusal motion, speedy trial motion, opposition brief, and preservation of objections are all filed
  • The mayor of Weehawken himself said the delays were excessive. The prosecution continued regardless.

04The Facts
What actually happened.
Mukamal approached Sara on September 2, 2024. She filed a police report that same afternoon — before he filed anything. The video he claimed to have does not exist. The quote he attributed to Manuel does not appear on the recording.
The Encounter
Sara filed the police report first — the same day as the incident.
At 5:16 PM on September 2, 2024, Sara and Manuel filed police report CAD 24-29264 — the same day as the incident, before Mukamal filed anything. Nineteen days later, Mukamal filed his complaint. Eleven days after receiving his own Notice to Cease from the building.
The Recording
The audio recording contradicts the complainant's account.
Audio diarization (Deepgram, Feb 24, 2026) confirms Mukamal said: "The whole building will know about you, buddy." The quote he attributed to Manuel — "I am going to ruin you like we did in the last building" — does not appear anywhere on the recording.
The Video
The video evidence the complainant claimed to have was never produced.
Mukamal claimed to have video of the incident. On December 2, 2025, Phyllis Walsh, Discovery Coordinator of the Weehawken Prosecutor's Office, confirmed in writing: "Our office has not received any video recordings from Brian Mukamal."
The Perjury
The complainant's absence claim is contradicted by building records.
On February 26, 2025, Mukamal claimed to be outside the country and unable to attend court. Gym surveillance footage and key fob access records place him in the building that day. The court accepted his verbal claim with no documentation.
The True Purpose
A mutual dismissal was proposed at mediation — linking both cases.
At the February 7, 2025 mediation, Mukamal's counsel proposed: drop Weehawken charges against Sara/Manuel in exchange for Sara dropping her North Bergen spitting complaint (Case 0908-S-2024-269). Sara refused. This reveals the complaint's true purpose: leverage.
The Charges
The alleged conduct: a reactive comment. No threats. No contact.
Sara's documented conduct during the incident: a reactive comment about Mukamal's age after he approached her and taunted her. No physical contact. No threats. No prior criminal record. Sara twice told Manuel "don't talk to him" — actively de-escalating the encounter.

Lack of Probable Cause

Disorderly Conduct — N.J.S.A. 2C:33-2

Requires: purpose to cause public inconvenience, annoyance or alarm through fighting/threatening/violent behavior or creating a hazardous condition.

The record: Sara did not approach Mukamal. He approached her. Her verbal response was reactive. No physical contact. No threats. De-escalating language is on the recording.

Harassment — N.J.S.A. 2C:33-4

Requires: purpose to harass through offensively coarse language, offensive touching, or a course of alarming conduct.

The record: "How old are you? 50? Grow up" — said once, reactively — is not offensively coarse language. A single encounter is not a "course of alarming conduct."

Judge announced she was "going to say they caused the delay" before Sara was heard on either motion. LoFaro: "I think it's clear what the ruling should be." Judge: "Absolutely." — after reviewing only LoFaro's papers. [Transcript 13:10–13:28]. Canon 3(B)(5); Canon 3(E)(1).

Only after this extended on-record verification did the judge state: “I am prepared to rule on the motion, but I do need to give them an opportunity to review it.” [13:02]. The court was still prepared to proceed on a motion its own staff had just confirmed was never served — until LoFaro himself stopped it, warning that ruling without Sara having seen the motion would be “an immediate appeal.” [13:10]. The restraint came from LoFaro’s tactical calculation, not any concern for Sara’s rights.

Throughout the verification, whenever Sara attempted to speak to confirm she had not received the motion, the judge silenced her: “I’m going to say one more time, if you don’t mind — not speaking because we’re on the record.” [06:08]. The defendant who had just been proven right was told to stop talking while the court investigated whether she was telling the truth. Canon 3(B)(4); Canon 3(E)(1).

"I don't see anything when I was going through the file. I don't see a complaint for contempt." [Transcript 17:14–17:25]. The contempt proceeding is on the court's own docket: initiated April 16, 2025; rescheduled to August 20, 2025; continued by court instruction. Canon 3(B)(1); Canon 3(B)(8).
06 Ex parte communications observed — Mar 18, 2026 +
Sara observed the former municipal prosecutor — holding no formal role — moving between LoFaro and Judge Olivieri and appearing to brief the judge on the case outside Sara's participation. ACJC complaint requests investigation. Canon 3(B)(7).
07 Repeated expression of preference for mutual dismissal — Jan 21 & Mar 18 +
"I've always thought that would be the best way to resolve this matter." [Transcript 21:28–21:59] — stated at both the January 21 and March 18 hearings. A judge presiding over a criminal matter may not express a personal preference for its resolution. Canon 3A(6).
08 Refusal to recognize Sara as pro se — Fall 2025 +
The court refused to acknowledge Sara as a pro se defendant because Mott had not filed the required formal withdrawal form. The court used Mott's paperwork failure to deny Sara standing to speak for herself. The failure was Mott's, not Sara's. R. 1:21-1(a); Canon 3(B)(4).

In dismissing Sara's counter-complaint, Judge Olivieri cited the Twig case and specific NJSA provisions that were NOT in Lofaro's motion papers. The judge made legal arguments on behalf of the complainant's attorney that he did not make himself. This crosses from judicial to adversarial. Canon 3(B)(2); Canon 3(E)(1).
10 Speedy trial ruling blocked to avoid constitutional finding — Apr 22, 2026 +
After dismissing Sara's counter-complaint, Lofaro proposed a 'mutual dismissal' (nothing mutual — the judge had just killed Sara's only complaint). Sara asked for a ruling on her pending speedy trial motion. The judge refused — saying it was irrelevant because Lofaro offered to dismiss. The stakes: a speedy trial dismissal = with prejudice + constitutional violation on the record + foundation for civil rights/malicious prosecution lawsuit. Lofaro's voluntary dismissal = without prejudice + no record of wrongdoing. The court is steering the defendant away from a constitutional ruling to protect itself and Lofaro from liability. Sara was then forced into another hearing to 'think about it.' Canon 3(B)(1); N.J. Const. Art. I ¶ 10.
11 Judge contradicts own prior on-record confirmations of Lofaro as prosecutor — Apr 22, 2026 +
At the January 2026 and March 2026 hearings, Judge Olivieri confirmed on the record that Lofaro was acting as prosecutor — this is in the transcripts. At the April 22 hearing, when the authorization question was raised, the judge started to say Lofaro was 'granted authority' but pivoted to 'he hasn't prosecuted yet' — directly contradicting her own prior confirmations. Judge also raised State v. Storm, 141 N.J. 245 (1995) sua sponte — the case requiring formal authorization for private prosecution. Lofaro admitted he 'never submitted forms' per R. 7:8-7. Canon 3(B)(1); Canon 3(B)(4).
12 Coercive pressure to accept lesser relief — Apr 22, 2026 +
After Sara rejected the 'mutual dismissal' and demanded a ruling on her constitutional speedy trial motion, the judge refused to rule and instead ordered another hearing on May 20, telling Sara to 'think about it.' This is coercive: a defendant with a pending constitutional motion is told to abandon it and accept lesser relief, or face indefinite continuation of baseless charges. Canon 3(B)(4); Canon 3(B)(5).
"Viewed together, these incidents describe a proceeding in which a pro se litigant is demeaned for representing herself, silenced when she speaks, disbelieved when she is corroborated, excluded from the court's communications with opposing counsel, and denied the constitutional protections that are the floor of every criminal proceeding in this State." — ACJC Complaint v4, March 21, 2026
Speedy Trial — Barker v. Wingo Analysis
Factor 1
Length of Delay
590+ days on a petty disorderly persons offense with a maximum $500 fine. 17 appearances. No trial date ever set. Presumptively prejudicial.
Factor 2
Reason for Delay
Calendar built around LoFaro's Camden County trial and family vacation. 7-month unexplained gap. Court refused to rule on speedy trial motion pre-trial. Mayor intervened — to no effect. At April 22 hearing, court refused to rule on speedy trial because Lofaro offered voluntary dismissal — steering defendant away from constitutional remedy to protect court and complainant's counsel from liability.
Factor 3
Assertion of Right
Asserted through prior counsel; in formal written motion Jan 24, 2026; in correspondence with Mayor Turner; in Preservation of Objections. Court's response: defer to trial — which is itself constitutional error. At April 22 hearing, court explicitly refused to rule — told Sara to 'think about' accepting voluntary dismissal instead.
Factor 4
Prejudice
$40,000+ in prior legal fees. Federal security clearance at risk. 590+ days of oppressive pretrial anxiety on a charge lacking probable cause.
